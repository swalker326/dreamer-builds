SHARDdecipher a broken tablet in a language no one has spoken

SHARD

A fragment of clay tablet survives with a few lines of an unknown language, each with a scribe’s English gloss. Every tablet is a brand-new language, generated from the day’s seed: its own sounds, its own script, its own word order and grammar.

Translate three lines, each harder than the last. Tap tiles to build your answer (tap a placed tile to take it back), then Carve. Every tile is judged: green = right tile, right place; ochre = in the answer but elsewhere; grey = not in the answer. Five tries per line.

Small tiles with a dash are affixes that attach to a word (they come before or after it, as the dash shows). Everything you need is somewhere on the tablet: compare lines that share a word, notice what changes when the English says wolves instead of wolf, and watch where the verb sits. Hover or tap a glyph to light up every copy of it.

Each glyph is one syllable. At the end the tablet’s grammar is revealed.
