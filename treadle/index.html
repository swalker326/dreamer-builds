How a draft works

A weaving draft is the sheet music of a loom. Four grids describe the cloth completely:

Threading(top) Each column is one warp end; the mark says which shaft its heddle hangs on. Shaft 1 is the bottom row, nearest the weaver. Tie-up(corner) Each column is a treadle; marks say which shafts that treadle lifts (rising-shed / jack loom). Treadling(right) Each row is one pass of the shuttle — a pick — and shows which treadle is pressed. Drawdown(middle) The cloth itself. A cell shows the warp color if that end's shaft is lifted on that pick; otherwise the weft passes over it.

In other words: warp up = tieup[ shaft(end) ][ treadle(pick) ]. Everything — plain weave, twills, diamonds, overshot, houndstooth, tartan — comes from that one rule plus the order of colored yarns.

Try it: drag a diagonal across the threading, press Tromp as writ (treadle in the same order you threaded), and watch a diamond bloom. Shift-click in the treadling to press two treadles at once.

Playing the cloth. The drawdown is read like a music-box roll. The shuttle sweeps one pick per beat, alternating direction as a real shuttle does. Wherever a warp end rises to the surface it plucks a note on a pentatonic scale — pitch from its shaft and its position across the warp — and the note rings for as long as the warp float lasts. A change of weft color strikes a low string. Plain weave chatters, twills climb in scales, long overshot floats drone.

Presets follow traditional 4-shaft structures (plain weave, 2/2 twill, bird's eye point twill, rosepath 1-2-3-4-1-4-3-2, goose-eye extended point twill, Monk's belt blocks with tabby, houndstooth 4&4) plus an 8-shaft point-twill diamond and a twill tartan.
